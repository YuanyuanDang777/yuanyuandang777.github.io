Biography

Hi! I'm Yuanyuan Dang (党媛媛), a Professor and Doctoral Supervisor at the School of Business Administration, South China University of Technology (SCUT). I am a recipient of the National Young Talent award.

I received my Ph.D. from Harbin Institute of Technology, with joint doctoral training at the University of Minnesota. My research sits at the intersection of healthcare service management, information systems, and econometrics, focusing on smart healthcare operations, digital health, Internet hospitals, and incentive mechanism design, studied through randomized field experiments, natural experiments, and causal inference methods.

My work has been published in leading journals including MIS Quarterly and Information Systems Research (both UTD24 journals), as well as Journal of Medical Internet Research, JMIR mHealth and uHealth, Information Technology & People, Electronic Markets, and 《管理科学学报》. I have served as Principal Investigator of two projects funded by the National Natural Science Foundation of China (NSFC).

Beyond academia, I serve as Deputy Director of the Guangdong Provincial Key Laboratory of Smart Healthcare Service Management (Guangdong Department of Education), lead the establishment of the School of Science and Technology Business at SCUT, and co-founded Baiyun Health Technology Co., Ltd., where I act as product manager — connecting research on digital health with real-world healthcare delivery.

MISQ2022

Anindya Ghose, Xitong Guo, Beibei Li, Yuanyuan Dang

MIS Quarterly, 46(1): 151–192, Mar 2022 UTD24

Empowering Patients Using Smart Mobile Health Platforms: Evidence of a Randomized Field Experiment

A randomized field experiment with 1,070 diabetes patients on a major mHealth platform in Asia, showing that mHealth adoption improves health behaviors and both short- and long-term healthcare outcomes, and that mobile (versus PC) delivery amplifies these effects.

Delivering Healthcare Through Teleconsultations: Implications for Offline Healthcare Disparity

Examines how telemedicine consultations rebalance geographic healthcare disparity, with implications for the design of telehealth programs and offline resource allocation.
